A **CVE** stands for **Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures**, which is a globally recognized dictionary of publicly known cybersecurity flaws. In the context of the **[EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng)**, CVEs serve as the standardized tracking mechanism that manufacturers use to catalog, reference, and report vulnerabilities found in "products with digital elements". [1](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/explained.html), [2](https://star.global/posts/cyber-resilience-act-article-14-reporting-requirements/), [3](https://openssf.org/public-policy/eu-cyber-resilience-act/), [4](https://www.craact.eu/regulation/cra/fulltext-and-download-pdf/), [5](https://legit.eu/resources/blog/your-simple-guide-to-common-vulnerabilities-and-exposures/)]

**Article 14** of the CRA legally mandates that manufacturers **must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe security incidents** to European authorities within strict, time-bound windows. It is the earliest major piece of the CRA to take effect, applying from **September 11, 2026**. [1](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html), [2](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rolandatoui_article-14-of-the-cyber-resilience-act-is-activity-7467833595341115393-GTJK), [3](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary), [4](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/reporting.html)

What Exactly Does Article 14 State?

Article 14 establishes a rigid, multi-stage reporting timeline for manufacturers. Notifications must be submitted via a single reporting platform simultaneously to the designated national Computer Security Incident Response Team (**CSIRT**) and the EU cybersecurity agency (**ENISA**). [1](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting), [2](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_14.html), [3](https://star.global/posts/cyber-resilience-act-article-14-reporting-requirements/)

The text breaks down specific reporting requirements into two major categories:

1. Actively Exploited Vulnerabilities

If a manufacturer becomes aware of a security flaw in their product that is actively being targeted or exploited in the wild, they must follow the **24-hour / 72-hour / 14-day clock**: [1](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting)

- **Within 24 Hours (Early Warning):** Submit an initial alert stating that an actively exploited vulnerability exists, including whether it poses a systemic risk. [1](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_14.html), [2](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rolandatoui_article-14-of-the-cyber-resilience-act-is-activity-7467833595341115393-GTJK), [3](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/reporting.html)
- **Within 72 Hours (Vulnerability Notification):** Provide a deeper update specifying the general characteristics of the flaw, its risk level, any known exploits, and any initial corrective or mitigating measures taken. , [2](https://star.global/posts/cyber-resilience-act-article-14-reporting-requirements/), [3](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/reporting.html), [4](https://star.global/posts/cyber-resilience-act-article-14-reporting-requirements/), [5](https://www.orbiqhq.com/eu-regulations/cyber-resilience-act)
- **Within 14 Days of a Fix (Final Report):** Submit a comprehensive report once a patch or corrective measure is available. This must detail the vulnerability, its root cause, and how users can apply the fix. [1](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting), [2](https://avatao.com/eu-cyber-resilience-act-developer-guide/)

2. Severe Incidents Impacting Product Security

Manufacturers must use the same phased framework to report any major security incident that affects the digital product’s integrity, safety, or availability:

- **Within 24 Hours:** Provide an early warning indicating that a severe incident has occurred.
- **Within 72 Hours:** Submit an incident notification detailing the severity, impact, and initial mitigation actions.
- **Within 1 Month:** Submit a final closing report analyzing the incident's root cause and impact. [1](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting), [2](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rolandatoui_article-14-of-the-cyber-resilience-act-is-activity-7467833595341115393-GTJK), [3](https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/regulation.html), [4](https://star.global/posts/cyber-resilience-act-article-14-reporting-requirements/), [5](https://ccb.belgium.be/regulation/cra)

Critical Structural Rules Under Article 14

- **Simultaneous Submission:** All notifications go through an electronic single reporting platform managed by ENISA to prevent double-reporting. [1](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting), [2](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_14.html)
- **Retrospective Power:** The obligation applies to products already available on the EU market, even if they were placed on the market before the full CRA text takes effect in late 2027. , [2](https://site.unibo.it/cybersecurity-legal-lab/en/cyber-resilience-act-guide-lab/cyber-resilience-act-guide/@@download/file/Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20-%20Guide.pdf)
- **Fines for Violations:** Failing to comply with Article 14 reporting requirements can trigger administrative fines up to **€15 million or 2.5% of global annual turnover**, whichever is higher. [1](https://www.aegister.com/en/cms/insights/cyber-resilience-act-cra-obligations-manufacturers/), [2](https://spac-alliance.org/library/regulations/cra/cyber-resilience-act-text/)
